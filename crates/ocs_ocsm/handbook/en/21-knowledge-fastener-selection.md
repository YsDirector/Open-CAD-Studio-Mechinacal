# 21 Knowledge: Fastener Selection and Locking

> English translation of `handbook/21-知识-紧固件选型与防松.md` (the Chinese original is the source of truth).

> This is the part where "the command does not decide for you": **choosing the bolt grade / length / whether to add a plain washer, a spring washer or a double nut**.
> The command side only executes the part chain you give it (see `04-bolt-joint.md`).
> The crushing check on this page **is computable**: companion script `tools/crush_check.py` (in this directory); every table here is generated by it.

## 0. Design Order (**read this first**: top-down, never the other way round)

**Two entry points** (decide first): the drawing has an **already given hole** and you only add the bolt → **reverse engineering** (class A, derive the spec from the hole diameter / plate thickness, see `13-task-kickoff-protocol.md` §1);
otherwise → **design from scratch** (class B): **the first step is to ask the user for the design inputs** (load / environment / material / constraints / deliverable, see `13` §2), then work down layer by layer.

The real design chain of a threaded connection runs **from the working conditions towards the drawing**; the drawing (the bolt joint this plugin draws) is only the last step that lands it:

```
① Working conditions   gearbox: radial / axial force, tilting moment; cylinder / pressure vessel: sealing contact pressure → will it leak?
                    ↓ yields "the minimum clamping force / preload F_req required"
② Bolt spec and grade  number n × size M × grade → per-bolt preload F_M (strength: σ = F/A_s ≤ ~0.9 R_p0.2)
                    ↓
③ Joint-member bearing check   p = F_M / A_p ≤ p_G (**§4 of this page**)
                    ↓ this step often decides "whether to add a plain washer / a flange part, change the material, add holes"
④ Tightening torque and method allowed F = min(F_bolt, F_base material) → T ≈ K·F·d (**§5**), then pick torque method / turn-of-nut / hydraulic tensioning
                    ↓
⑤ Material and heat treatment  bolt grade ↔ heat treatment / surface treatment; heat-treat the joint members to avoid crushing and deformation (see 23-knowledge)
                    ↓
⑥ Drawing (OCSM commands)  drop the part chain in the XY view: `OCSMJOINT` (see 04-bolt-joint.md)
```

**Two rules of thumb (calibration the user made on 2026-09-15)**:

1. **First the bolt, then the hole** — not the other way round. The hole (through-hole diameter) is decided by the **bolt spec + assembly accuracy + machining method**,
   and must land on **standard drill sizes** and the position tolerance of the drilled hole (see §4.11).
2. **Bolt strength is usually higher than the base-material strength → the actual tightening torque is often controlled by the base material (joint members)**:
   it is not "tighten as hard as the bolt can take" but
   `allowed preload F = min(F_M of the bolt by grade, allowed value of the base material by p_G)`.
   On soft materials this limit is often **far below** the bolt's design preload (the reverse-calculation mode in §5 tells you directly who is in control).

## 1. The Three Things About a Threaded Connection (think them through before drawing)

1. **Strength**: bolt grade × preload ≥ working load (shear additionally needs a shear/bearing calculation).
2. **Bearing-face crushing**: the bolt head / nut bearing face presses on the joint members — soft materials (aluminium/plastic/thin plate) crush, collapse, the bolt sinks in
   → **add a plain washer** (or even flange bolts / a larger bearing face) or reduce the preload. Criterion in §4 (**this section is computable**).
3. **Locking**: the preload decays under vibration / impact / temperature change → choose a locking method (§2).

## 2. Locking Methods (how to draw them in OCSM)

| Method | Principle | OCSM part chain | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Spring washer** (GB/T 93) | The washer spreads open, its edge bites the bearing face + elastic compensation | `washer=washer_971:8 washer=washer_93:8 nut=nut_c41:8` | Most common; **plain washer inside, spring washer outside** (on the nut side) |
| **Double nut** (jammed together) | The two nuts jam; the engaged thread takes extra pressure → friction locking | `nut=nut_61721:8 nut=nut_c41:8` (thin inside, thick outside) or `nut=nut_c41:8 nut=nut_c41:8` (two thick nuts) | Reliable and removable; takes axial space (recalculate the length) |
| **Head-drilled bolt + split pin / lock wire** | Mechanical locking (does not rely on friction) | `bolt=hex_bolt_hole_a:8 …` (GB/T 32.1) | Strong vibration / safety critical; **split pins / lock wire are not in the library yet, draw them separately** |
| Nylon / all-metal lock nut | Non-metal insert or a crimp produces friction | Not in the library yet → substitute an existing family or draw by hand | Common and removable |
| Thread-locking adhesive | Chemical locking | Not shown in the geometry (write it into the technical requirements) | Common for small parts / thin walls |
| Tab washer | Mechanical stop | ✅ In the library: `lock_washer_858` (with round nut `round_nut_812`); how to draw: `OCSMPART lock_washer_858 <d> <s>` | Used with a round nut |
| Slotted nut + split pin | Mechanical stop | ❌ Not in the library yet (families to add: slotted nut GB/T 6178 series, split pin GB/T 91) | Used with a head-drilled bolt |

**How to choose**: general equipment → spring washer (or lock nut); clear vibration / impact → double nut or lock nut + plain washer;
safety critical, must not come loose → head-drilled bolt + split pin; aluminium / thin plate / plastic → **a plain washer is almost mandatory**, and reduce the tightening torque at the same time.

## 3. Length Rule (OCSM's algorithm — you must give the right part chain)

```
required l ≥ Σ part heights + exposed thread count × pitch      (1 thread = 1 pitch; default exposure 2.5 threads)
take the smallest value ≥ the requirement from the family's **table supply-length series** ("slightly long is fine")
```

- There must be **2–3 threads** outside the nut face; an over-long exposure (>5 threads) is neither good-looking nor economical.
- What is exposed must be **thread**: a fully threaded bolt (GB/T 5783) is the least trouble; for a partly threaded one (5780/5782) check that `b` (thread length) is enough —
  if the threaded portion does not cover the nut = **you cannot screw it on** (switch to a fully threaded family, or lengthen it until it covers).
- For measured values (M8 coarse thread, steel) see the "length quick reference" in `04-bolt-joint.md`; **the supply series follows the table** (e.g. class C M8 has 35).

## 4. Crushing (bearing-face compressive stress) Check — Computable

### 4.1 Criterion

```
p = F_M / A_p  ≤  p_G
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `F_M` | assembly preload (N) | see the `F_M` column of Table A in §4.3 (= p × A_p from the source table, at 90% R_p0.2, μ_G=0.12) |
| `A_p` | bearing-face area (mm²) | see §4.2: computed from the head type / with or without washer, **not** the bolt's nominal area |
| `p_G` | **allowable surface pressure** of the joint-member material (N/mm²) | see Table C in §4.6 (VDI 2230 table values) |

Premises of the source table values (correct them if exceeded, see §4.8): **room temperature, no chamfer on the through hole, sufficiently large joint-member outer diameter**.

### 4.2 The Three Bearing Faces (how A_p is computed)

| Case | A_p | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hex head class A/B** (GB/T 5782/5783), **type 1 / thin nut** (GB/T 6170/6172.1) | `π/4 (dw² − dh²)` | Has a **washer face** dw (`dw` in the table / Table A in §4.3) |
| **Class C head** (GB/T 5780), **class C nut** (GB/T 41) | `(√3/2)·s² − π/4·dh²` | **No washer face** → the full hex end face bears (largest area) |
| **Socket head cap screw** (GB/T 70.1 / ISO 4762) | `π/4 (dw² − dh²)` | dw see Table B in §4.5 |
| **With a plain washer added** (GB/T 97.1) | `π/4 (d2² − dh²)` | d2 = washer outer diameter; **the pressure drops to about 1/3** (Table D in §4.7) |

`dw`, `s` and `d2` can all be taken from the **plugin parts library** (`GET /api/parts`, or `crates/ocs_ocsm/src/tables/*.json` in this repository) —
the handbook's table values are aligned from there plus the source references.

### 4.8 Corrections and Premises (do not ignore)

| Factor | Effect | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hole-mouth chamfer** | The allowable value may go **+20%~25%** (bearing effect) | Steel parts may be chamfered; use +20% in the calculation |
| **Power (electric/pneumatic) tightening** | The allowable value may go **−25%** (large preload scatter) | Multiply by 0.75, or switch to a torque wrench / hydraulic tensioning |
| **Coarse through hole** | `dh` gets bigger → `A_p` gets smaller → **the pressure rises** (M8: hole Ø9 → 442; hole Ø10 → 686) | Choose a **finer** series per ISO 273 / GB/T 5277 |
| **Thin wall / insufficient outer diameter** | The p_G table values no longer hold (the material ring cannot take it) | Enlarge the boss / add a washer / reduce the preload |
| Temperature | At high temperature the material softens and the preload relaxes | Derate by the high-temperature allowable values (not included in these tables) |
| Surface roughness / perpendicularity | Affects the contact area and preload stability | See the table below |

Surface and form requirements for the contact faces (DIN 4768 / ISO 4288, by thread size):

### 4.9 The Washer Hardness Class Must Match the Bolt Grade (ISO 887)

| Bolt grade | ≤6.8 | 8.8 | 9.8 | 10.9 | 12.9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 HV washer | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| 200 HV washer | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| 300 HV washer | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |

- **Grade 8.8 must not use a 100 HV soft washer** (the washer is crushed into a bell mouth and the preload decays immediately);
  **10.9 needs 300 HV**; **12.9 must not use an ordinary plain washer at all** (it needs a specially hardened washer).
- Our library: `washer_971` (GB/T 97.1) is in the **200 HV class** (corresponding to ISO 7089),
  **usable for grade 8.8 ✓**; for 10.9 and above choose a 300 HV washer. GB/T 95 (class C, 100 HV class) does not match 8.8.
  (Please re-check the standard correspondence of the hardness classes against your supplier data once.)

### 4.10 Three Worked Examples (all reproducible with the script)

```bash
cd ~/.agents/skills/ocsm-manual/manual/tools

# ① Steel-steel: M8 8.8, Q235, medium hole Ø9 → passes (442 ≤ 490, very tight)
python3 crush_check.py M8 8.8 hex_ab mat=Q235

# ② Aluminium part: M8 8.8, 6061-T6, coarse-fitting hole Ø10 → 1.9× over the limit ✗, the script offers a plain-washer fix
python3 crush_check.py M8 8.8 hex_ab mat=6061 dh=10

# ③ Higher preload: M10 10.9 socket head, 6061-T6 → over the limit; 198 ✓ after adding a plain washer Ø20
python3 crush_check.py M10 10.9 socket mat=AlMgSi dh=11
```

**Conclusions (important)**:
- **On steel (Q235), grade 8.8 is only "just" sufficient at M6–M10**: M6 364, M8 442, M10 405 (≤490 ✓);
  **from M12 it exceeds the limit** (M12 580, M16 515, M20 532, M24 528, M30 520 > 490 ✗) —
  because the large-size through hole (ISO 273 medium) eats too much of the washer face. So large bolts on soft steel must also **add a plain washer or chamfer the hole mouth**.
  Whether steel parts need a plain washer at all: for M6–M10 it mainly depends on **locking and protecting the bearing face**; from M12 up, run the crushing calculation first.
- **On aluminium / plastic / thin plate, a grade 8.8 bolt almost always exceeds the limit** → **a plain washer is mandatory** (M8: 442 → 135 ✓),
  or use flange bolts / a larger bearing face / a lower tightening torque.
- **A bigger hole means higher pressure** (M8: Ø9 → 442; Ø10 → 686) → fix the through hole per §4.11: **bolt first, hole second**,
  keep the hole in the medium/fine series as far as possible; do not open it up just to make assembly easier.
- **The higher the bolt grade the harder it presses**: 10.9 is 47% above 8.8, 12.9 is 72% above → on soft materials, going to a higher grade is actually more dangerous.
- **If you want a torque rather than a pass/fail**: use the reverse-calculation mode of §5 (`--allow`) to get `F_allow` and `T_max`;
  it tells you whether the torque limit comes from the **bolt** or from the **base material** (on soft materials it is almost always the base material).
- For a quick judgement: look up the `p(grade)` value in §4.3 and compare it with the `p_G` of the joint-member material in §4.6 (no hand calculation needed).

### 4.11 How to Fix the Hole (through hole) Diameter: **bolt → hole**

The hole diameter is a **design variable**, not "the drawing happens to have a hole first"; it affects three things at once: assembly feasibility, the position-tolerance requirement, and the **bearing area (crushing)**.

1. **Target hole diameter**: pick the ISO 273 / GB/T 5277 series by assembly accuracy (fine / medium / coarse):

   | Thread | fine fitting | **medium fitting** | coarse fitting |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | M8 | 8.4 | **9** | 10 |
   | M10 | 10.5 | **11** | 12 |
   | M12 | 13 | **13.5** | 14 |
   | M16 | 17 | **17.5** | 18 |

   (M4 4.3/4.5/5, M5 5.3/5.5/6, M6 6.4/6.6/7 — the medium column is from the source table ✓; the fine/coarse columns are ±0.5~1 per the series;
   check large sizes against your own handbook's GB/T 5277 table.)
2. **Land on a drill / machining size**: a drilled hole carries a manufacturing deviation, and drills do not come in every diameter →
   **the actual hole diameter is the target diameter rounded up to an available standard drill / machining size**, and check the position tolerance of the drilled hole
   (holes misaligned between the two plates further reduce the effective bearing area).
3. **Re-run the crushing check with the actual (oversize) hole**: the bigger `dh` → the smaller `A_p` → the higher `p`.
   Example: M8 8.8, hole Ø9 → 442; hole Ø10 → **686** (§4.3 Table A / §4.8).
4. **Trade-off**: a slightly bigger hole → easier assembly, a looser position tolerance (lower cost); the price is worse bearing.
   So **on soft materials prefer a smaller hole + a plain washer**, rather than "open the hole up so it is easy to assemble".
5. A fine-fitting hole (e.g. M12 fine 13) clearly improves the bearing (M12 8.8: medium hole 580 → fine hole 508),
   but assembly gets harder — that is decided by the assembly process and the batch size.

> Position taken: **first fix the bolt by strength / working conditions (size, grade, number), then fix the hole diameter by the assembly process, and finally re-run the crushing check with the actual hole diameter**;
> the other way round, "there is already a Ø10 hole, now find a bolt", only suits repair / reverse-engineering cases (there the hole diameter is a given condition, so you can only add a plain washer or drop a grade).

## 5. Tightening Torque and "Who Is in Control"

### 5.1 Allowed Preload: Take the Smaller of "bolt" and "base material"

```
F_allow = min( F_M (bolt, by grade/size) , p_G × A_p (base material, by bearing face) )
T ≈ K · F_allow · d          (K see the table below; for a detailed calculation decompose per VDI 2230's μ_G/μ_K)
```

| Friction / lubrication state | K | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dry, unlubricated (steel / zinc-plated) | **0.20** | Default; largest scatter |
| Zinc-plated (slightly sliding) | 0.18 | |
| Light oil lubrication | 0.15 | |
| MoS₂ lubrication | 0.14 | |
| Stainless-steel anti-seize paste / solid lubrication | 0.12 | |

**Reverse-calculation tool (recommended)**: `tools/crush_check.py --allow` (it also gives the allowed F, the T limit and who is in control)

```bash
python3 crush_check.py M8 8.8 --allow mat=6061 dh=10          # aluminium part: base-material controlled → F 9.8 kN, T ≈ 15.6 N·m
python3 crush_check.py M8 8.8 --allow mat=Q235 dh=9           # steel part: bolt controlled → F 18.6 kN, T ≈ 29.8 N·m
python3 crush_check.py M8 8.8 --allow mat=6061 dh=10 washer=97.1   # after adding a plain washer it is back to "bolt controlled" (T ≈ 29.8)
python3 crush_check.py M12 8.8 --allow mat=Q235 dh=13         # M12 medium hole: base-material controlled (4% short)
```

Exit codes: `0` = bolt controlled (the base material is sufficient); `2` = **base-material controlled** (the torque limit is not set by the bolt strength).

### 5.2 How Much Preload Is Needed? — decided by the higher-level working conditions (**knowledge skeleton, to be filled in step by step**)

| Case | Higher-level conditions | Required preload (skeleton) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gearbox / general machinery** | radial force, axial force, tilting moment, torque reaction | anti-slip: `F_req ≥ F_transverse / (μ · n · i)` (i = number of friction faces); anti-separation: `F_req ≥ F_axial / n + sealing/rigidity margin` |
| **Cylinder / pressure vessel (sealing)** | internal pressure p, the **sealing contact pressure** q required by the gasket / O-ring | `F_req ≥ q · A_sealing face / n`, and the contact pressure must still be held in service (the internal pressure "pushes the flange open" → the load factor must be computed) |
| Alternating load | fatigue | the preload must keep the bolt stress amplitude from getting too large (VDI 2230's R series) |
| Mainly location / locking | a shear pin / key shares the load | the preload may be set by the process; the emphasis is on locking |

> These rows are a **skeleton awaiting detailed calculation**: the full algorithms for gearboxes and cylinders (load sharing, flange stiffness, sealing contact pressure, temperature)
> will become a separate chapter later; when they land they connect to §5.1 of this page: `F_req → choose n, M, grade → check p_G → fix T`.

### 5.3 The Tightening Method Decides "How Much You Actually Get"

| Method | Actual preload scatter | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Torque method (ordinary wrench / impact) | ±30% or more | Large friction scatter; you can only use 60–70% of the design value |
| Torque method (calibrated torque wrench + lubrication control) | ±15~25% | Normal production |
| Turn-of-nut (yield-point control) | around ±10% | Tighten to snug first, then turn a fixed angle |
| Hydraulic tensioning / thermal elongation | ±5~10% | Large bolts, critical joints |

So **the value in a torque table is not an absolute "you must tighten to this"**, it is a "target value converted by method": where the scatter is large, either leave a bigger margin
or raise the method grade (this is also where "power tightening needs −25% on p_G" comes from, see §4.8).

## 6. Grades and Materials

| Grade | R_p0.2 min (MPa) | Rm min (MPa) | Typical use | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4.8 | 340 | 400 | general sheet metal / light load | low-carbon steel |
| 8.8 | **640** | 800 | **general machinery (the default first choice)** | quenched-and-tempered medium-carbon / alloy steel |
| 10.9 | **940** | 1000 | heavy load / critical | prone to hydrogen embrittlement (must be de-hydrogenated) |
| 12.9 | **1100** | 1200 | very high strength | quite brittle, use with care; no ordinary plain washer |

- Nut grade pairing: 8.8 with a grade 8 nut; 10.9 with grade 10 ("bolt grade ≥ nut grade").
- Stainless steel: A2-70/A4-70 are common; a stainless bolt is weaker than an 8.8 of the same size and **seizes easily** (apply anti-seize paste).
- Rough tightening-torque estimate: `T ≈ K · F_M · d`, dry steel K≈0.2 → M8 8.8: `0.2 × 18608 × 8 ≈ 29.8 N·m`.
  (For a finer K take it by lubrication / coating, see VDI 2230; **torque-wrench accuracy** decides the preload scatter.)

## 7. Empirical Data

- Through hole: ISO 273 / GB/T 5277 — medium fitting M8 → **Ø9**, M10 → Ø11, M12 → Ø13.5, M16 → Ø17.5
  (coarse fitting is one size bigger each: M8 → Ø10, M10 → Ø12, M12 → Ø14, M16 → Ø18).
- Bearing-face diameters: M8 class A/B head `dw = 11.6`; class C head has no washer face (full hex end face s=13); plain washer 97.1 Ø8 outer diameter `d2 = 16`.
- On aluminium alloy (6061-T6, Rm≈290, p_G=360) an M8 8.8 exceeds the limit 1.9× → **a plain washer is mandatory** (→135 ✓).
- Cast iron (HT250, p_G=850) resists crushing even better than steel (cast iron allows a high local bearing pressure) → on cast-iron parts you normally do not add a washer for crushing.
